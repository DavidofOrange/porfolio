01 — Philosophy

We don't build guitars to a schedule. We wait for the right board, listen to how it rings, and carve until the top moves like water under a lamp. Then we polish, and wait, and polish again — because some things only arrive slowly.

03 — Craft

Nine months, six rituals.

Every Maple guitar passes through the same hands from the first cut to the last string. Nothing is outsourced, nothing is rushed.

  1. 01

    Week 1

    Choosing the tonewood

    We tap, weigh and flex every board, keeping only tops that ring long and clear. The rest go back to the rack to age another year.

  2. 02

    Weeks 2–4

    Bookmatching & joining

    The board is split and opened like a book, so each half mirrors the other. A hide-glue joint makes the seam all but disappear.

  3. 03

    Weeks 5–9

    Carving the top

    Gouges and thumb planes shape the arch by eye and by touch, sweeping the figure into the light like a dune.

  4. 04

    Weeks 10–14

    Neck & fretwork

    The neck is carved to your hand. Twenty-two medium-jumbo frets are seated, levelled and crowned by hand, then polished to glass.

  5. 05

    Weeks 15–26

    The finish

    Hand-mixed aniline stains, then fourteen thin coats of nitrocellulose, each rubbed back by hand. Eleven weeks for a skin thinner than paper.

  6. 06

    Final week

    Setup & voicing

    Pickups are wound to match the wood, the nut is cut to the hundredth of a millimetre, and the guitar is played for a week before it ships.
